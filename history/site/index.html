Fern at the Ready v2 (Burn 2)FAIL

Bouncing Ball — the face-free control (Burn 2)PARTIAL

2026-11-21

The three-material sequence arrived in order with distinct physics (wood flip-and-settle, brass high springy arc, paper tumble-and-settle) and the exact named end state. Unverified then: decaying multi-bounce and contact-frame squash.

Fern at the Ready v3 — the arm-only test (Burn 3)FAIL

2026-11-21

What we learned — The hypothesis splits. Motion restriction delivered the gesture exactly (hammer raised at mid, back down at end, camera never moved) — but the buns re-drew into curls by about 1.25 s anyway, and an un-named closed-eye grin closed the clip. Refined law: any motion of the character herself triggers the hair re-draw; anchors hold only when she is fully still and the world moves around her.

Keeper's Welcome v3 — designing for the push-in (Burn 3)PARTIAL

2026-11-21

What we learned — From a key with Waverly at about one-sixth of frame height the push-in was total, but painterly throughout and on-model at the landing. The landing was invented, not designed: her rolled scroll became an unrolled scroll covered in pseudo-writing. Landings are found compositions needing their own QC; their characteristic defect is invented props, text-bearing above all.

Bouncing Ball v2 (Burn 3)FAIL

2026-11-21

What we learned — The three-prop key betrayed the test: the generator animated a duplicate token while the key's own token never moved, and the bounce re-launched to full height after settling. The indicted vector was the key, not the generator — a multi-prop still is not a single-object key.

Bouncing Ball v3 — single-object key (Burn 4)FAIL

2026-11-21

What we learned — The dedicated single-object key passed as a key, but the clip turned the disc into a round ball, flew a wandering arc, then duplicated it into floating copies. Object permanence cannot be prompt-held in a minimal scene.

Bouncing Ball v4 — permanence language (Burn 4)FAIL

2026-11-21

What we learned — Explicit permanence language changed the failure mode, not the outcome: the token morphed in place (loaf to dome to ring to oval) and scale-pulsed instead of bouncing. "Squash" wording invites morphing. After four attempts the exercise changes instrument: the drawn route, where spacing and squash are exact by construction.

Fern at the Ready v4 — two keys, lowering (Burn 4)FAIL

2026-11-21

What we learned — Frame 0 was not the start key — the generator re-staged the scene entirely; the end matched the end key's pose thematically, not its composition. Refined law: endpoints hold only when both keys are generator-native to one scene instance.

Keeper's Welcome v4 (Burn 4)PARTIAL

2026-11-21

What we learned — Text discipline held completely — no text or pseudo-writing anywhere — and the nod landed. But Waverly walked to camera, broke the room's scale, and arrived in a bust close-up wearing invented jewelry. A landing cannot be designed from the start key while the character is free to move.

The Gates

The toll gates: full reproductions of public-domain films, in order. A gate passes only on a prepass plus a full render, frame-compared.

Gate 1 — Steamboat Willie (1928): the prepassDRAFT

2026-11-21

What we learned — Setup and prepass complete; the gate is NOT passed — a gate passes only on prepass plus full render. Sixty hand-drawn repro pages at real shot timing, from an arc sketch derived from the original's actual shots. The validator then checked every page mechanically: 30 PASS, 30 FLAG. Its root-cause finding: the renderer's answer to solid black is a flat wash with interior ink density 0.00 on all 60 pages — the mechanical form of "doesn't look good yet". The flagged pages are the re-draw queue, to be re-drawn as key frames in the Emise design language.

Live Drawing

A conversation, drawn in real time on white paper — practiced every session of Wavelet 4 for the Dec 4 performance. Each drawing keeps its white ground inside the picture itself.

Live Drawing, Session 1 — studio pacePASS

2026-11-22 · EE 72

What we learned — Skeleton: one light, lit slowly — a full third of the drawing is the single lantern — then a street of houses, then the windows answering, then the sentence written underneath everything. What remained when the night was drawn: ignition, answering, inscription, in that order. The idea the drawing itself generated: the words are not a caption. They sit at street level, under the scene, spanning its full width — the drawing puts inscription as the ground the town stands on. And the time budget was its own evidence: as long on one lantern as on the whole street — the conversation's weight was the first light, not the town.

Live Drawing, Session 2 — meeting pacePASS

2026-11-22 · EE 87

What we learned — What survived the 4x compression: the lantern entire, the five-house roofline, the answering windows, and the full sentence — the skeleton is unchanged at a quarter of the time; what survived speed is the conversation itself. What fell away: eave hatching, window crossbars, two of five stars, the wind curls, the crescent's inner arc, the glow's slow build — all duration, no meaning, except the moon's arc. The idea the drawing generated: written fast by hand, the sentence changes role. At studio pace it was a caption revealed after the scene; stroked at speed it lands as the scene's last act — the hand signs the drawing.
