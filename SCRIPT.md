# Scrolltown: The Great Form Mix-Up

**A Saturday-morning cartoon short for the Roads to the Future suite**
Format: single self-contained web cartoon · 6 scenes · ~4–6 minutes on autoplay · every line captioned (fully enjoyable muted) · no audio, no external assets
Narrator's frame: a kid talks straight to the viewer, opens and closes the show, and rides a number-cart between the neighborhoods of the Great Scroll.

---

## The World (kid translation of the Exocortexia suite)

Scrolltown is the town inside the **Great Scroll**. Every citizen has their **own little house-scroll** (their phext) holding their stories, forms, and important papers. Big **page-houses** serve everyone. Doors open only for neighbors carrying the **right key-badge** (roles and claims). The five town places are the real Exocortexia apps, in kid terms:

| Town place | What it does | The real app |
|---|---|---|
| The Records Desk | The library that finds any paper | Records Desk |
| The Minutes Tower | Rings a bell and writes down what everyone decided | Minutes Publisher |
| The Ledger Counting House | Counts every token fairly, out loud | Contract & Grant Ledger |
| The Catalog Map Room | A map of everything in town | Open Data Catalog |
| The Forms Router Post Tower | Sends every form to the right house | Forms Intake Router |

---

## Cast

- **Fern** — our narrator, 8 years old. Lives at 1.1.1/1.1.1/1.1.1, Home Street. Holds the **Keeper** gift: checks key-badges so every paper goes only to its own house.
- **Wren** — crew. Gift: **Finder**. "If it's hiding, I'll spot it."
- **Theo** — crew. Gift: **Sorter**. Loves a messy pile.
- **Bea** — crew. Gift: **Carrier**. Carries everything safe and sound; nothing drops.
- **Ledger** — the Counting House bot. A friendly robot who transforms to help: rolling cart → bridge → stairs. Transformation is always in service of a neighbor. Says "Beep" the way other people say "period."
- **Mrs. Hen** — a neighbor. Her garden permit went missing in the mix-up.
- **Grandpa** — Fern's grandpa. His best story, written on one soft old page, is somewhere in the scatter.
- Town keepers (one-line cameos): **Ms. Folio** (Records Desk), **Mr. Bell** (Minutes Tower), **Ms. Atlas** (Catalog Map Room), **Mr. Route** (Forms Router Post Tower).

## Scene 1 — "Hi, Neighbor" (Home Street)

*Staging: Morning light. A cozy street of little scroll-houses with rounded roofs. Fern stands at the gate of one small house with a curled-paper roof, waves straight at the viewer. Fern's house-scroll glows gently. Animation: house bobs softly; a butterfly crosses; Fern waves on a loop.*

**FERN** *(to viewer)*: Hi, neighbor. I'm Fern. I'm eight, and this is my town.
**FERN**: We live inside the Great Scroll. It's called Scrolltown.
**FERN**: See this little house? It's my house-scroll. My stories live here. My drawings live here. My important papers live here.
**FERN**: Every neighbor has their own house-scroll. And every door only opens for the key-badge that belongs to it.
**FERN**: Want to see the town? Hop on the number-cart. Hold on to your hat!

*Lesson riding inside: your own phext is yours; doors open by key-badge (roles & claims).*

## Scene 2 — "The Number-Cart Tour"

*Staging: Fern rides a small open cart along a path printed with big friendly numbers. Five stops, one joyful pass. Animation: cart rolls and bobs; at each stop the town place does its job in one motion — the Desk's drawer pops open with a paper, the Tower's bell swings, Ledger counts tokens that hop into a row, the Map Room's map unrolls, the Post Tower's chute delivers a form into a mailbox.*

**FERN**: The number-cart rides the numbers. Listen — it counts as it rolls!
**EVERYONE** *(soft chant)*: One… one… one… rolling home!
**FERN**: First stop: 1.2.1 — the Records Desk! The library that can find ANY paper.
**MS. FOLIO**: Lost a paper? Tell me its name. I'll find where it lives.
**FERN**: Next stop: the Minutes Tower!
**MR. BELL**: When the town decides something, I ring the bell and write it down — so nobody ever has to forget.
**FERN**: The Ledger Counting House!
**LEDGER**: I count every token. Out loud. Fair and square. Beep.
**FERN**: The Catalog Map Room!
**MS. ATLAS**: My map shows where everything in town lives. Everything!
**FERN**: And the Forms Router Post Tower!
**MR. ROUTE**: Every form that comes to my tower, I send it to the right house. Every single one.
**FERN**: Five places, one town, and every paper in its own home. That's Scrolltown!

*Lesson riding inside: the five services, one line each — retrieval, record, ledger, catalog, routing.*

## Scene 3 — "Ah… Ah… Achoo!"

*Staging: The Post Tower. Mr. Route's nose wiggles. A gentle, funny sneeze — the tower's cap wobbles like a hat. Forms flutter down like big soft snowflakes, never scary. Animation: pages flutter and drift; one tomato-stamped permit lands in a birdbath; a soft old page slides under a bench.*

**FERN** *(narrating)*: But today… today was a mixed-up day.
**MR. ROUTE**: Ah… ah… ah-CHOO!
**FERN**: The Post Tower sneezed! And all the forms flew EVERYWHERE!
**MRS. HEN** *(arriving, worried)*: Oh dear, oh dear. My garden permit! It was supposed to come to MY house. Has anyone seen it?
**FERN**: Don't worry, Mrs. Hen. Mixed-up is a feeling, and feelings are okay. We'll find it.
**FERN** *(quiet, to viewer)*: But I was worried too. Grandpa's story page was in the mail today. His best story. If it lands in the wrong house… nobody will know whose it is.
**FERN**: Crew! We have a case!

*Lesson riding inside: feelings named plainly; a routing failure is a problem to solve, not a villain to fight.*

## Scene 4 — "The Crew and the River of Pages"

*Staging: The crew gathers at the edge of the Scroll Valley. Loose pages drift past like a slow river — too wide to jump. Animation: each friend gets a one-beat gift moment (Wren's eyes sparkle-spot a far glint; Theo stack-taps a messy pile into neatness; Bea balances a tall stack without a wobble). Ledger rolls up as a cart, unfolds into a bridge across the page-stream. The crew crosses slowly, one at a time.*

**FERN**: When a neighbor has a problem, the crew comes. Wren finds things.
**WREN**: If it's hiding, I'll spot it. Finder — that's me!
**FERN**: Theo sorts things.
**THEO**: Give me a messy pile. I love a messy pile.
**FERN**: Bea carries things, safe and sound.
**BEA**: Nothing drops on my watch. Nothing.
**FERN**: And me? I'm the Keeper. I check the key-badges, so every paper goes only to its own house.
**WREN**: Uh-oh. The loose pages are a river today. The Scroll Valley stream is too wide to jump.
**LEDGER** *(rolling up)*: Beep. A bridge would help. I can help.
*(Ledger transforms: cart → bridge.)*
**FERN**: Ledger! You turned into a bridge!
**LEDGER**: Counting House bots help neighbors. That is the whole job. Beep.
**FERN**: Slow and steady, friends. One page at a time.

*Lesson riding inside: transformation in service of a neighbor; small gifts, introduced plainly.*

## Scene 5 — "Together, Just Right"

*Staging: The far bank. A big friendly pile of scattered forms. The crew works in rhythm — find, sort, carry, check — like a little dance. Animation: pages pass hand to hand down a line; four gift badges glow one by one, then all together; Mrs. Hen's tomato-stamped permit rises to the top of its pile. The soft old page is found last, under the pile.*

**WREN**: Finder eyes on! I see… a birthday card… a bus pass… and — there! A garden permit with a little tomato on it!
**THEO**: Sorter hands ready! Permits here, stories there, letters in the middle. Pile by pile, home by home.
**BEA**: Carrier coming through! Arms wide, papers safe. Nothing drops!
**FERN**: Keeper check! Mrs. Hen's badge — tomato stamp, Garden Street. This permit is hers. Badge matches!
**WREN**: Wait. One more page, under the pile. It's old. And soft. And it smells like Grandpa's soup.
**FERN** *(gently)*: It's Grandpa's story. The page about the night the stars fell into the lake.
**THEO**: Should we read it? Just a peek?
**FERN**: We never peek. It's not our house. Stories belong to their own house-scroll — we just carry them home.
**EVERYONE**: Finder finds! Sorter sorts! Carrier carries! Keeper keeps!
**FERN**: Alone, our gifts are little. Together? Together they're just right.

*Lesson riding inside: claims-checked delivery — and privacy played as kindness, not rules.*

## Scene 6 — "See You Tomorrow" (The Reading Tree)

*Staging: Late golden light under the Reading Tree. Mrs. Hen holds her permit like a trophy. Grandpa ambles in, unhurried. The crew sits in a half-circle. Animation: cart parked, numbers dimming like fireflies; leaves drift; the story page glows warm as it is read.*

**MRS. HEN**: My permit! My tomatoes thank you, crew.
**FERN**: And look — Grandpa, we found your story. We didn't peek. We carried it home.
**GRANDPA**: That's the way. Read it out loud, Fern. Stories are for sharing — when the owner says so.
**FERN** *(reading)*: "The night the stars fell into the lake, we didn't catch a single one. We just watched, together — and that was the whole treasure."
**FERN** *(to viewer)*: We found every paper. We fixed the mix-up. And you know what? It's okay to feel mixed-up sometimes. Grown-ups feel mixed-up too. What matters is: neighbors help, one page at a time.
**FERN**: The scroll is yours, neighbor. See you tomorrow.
*(The number-cart rolls off into the evening, counting softly.)*
**CART** *(fading)*: One… one… one…

*Lesson riding inside: consent makes sharing sweet; the power is handed to the viewer.*

## Playback & craft spec (for the build)

- **Structure**: 6 scenes, one SVG stage; each scene is a caption sequence — a caption bar shows one line at a time with the speaker's name; the stage animates gently behind it.
- **Controls**: big friendly **Prev / Next** buttons (≥48px), a **Play / Pause** autoplay toggle, scene dots or a scene strip, and **← / → arrow keys**. Autoplay advances when each caption's time is up (roughly 4–6 seconds per line, ~4–6 minutes total); any manual press pauses autoplay.
- **Captions**: every spoken line above is a caption. The cartoon is fully enjoyable muted. No audio files exist.
- **Motion**: simple loops — bobbing, a rolling cart, page flutter, Ledger's staged transformation (cart in Scene 2, bridge in Scene 4; stairs may appear in Scene 5 as he helps Bea reach the top of the pile). Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, loops calm to stills and advance stays manual-friendly.
- **Look**: Saturday Morning Breakfast palette — cream `#FFFBF0`, coral / powder sky / butter / mint pastels, warm charcoal ink, rounded shapes, gentle shadows. Flat-color inline SVG only. No external assets, no tracking, one file.
- **Cast look**: Fern (coral hoodie, key-badge on a string), Wren (sky-blue cap, magnifier), Theo (butter-yellow scarf, stack of papers), Bea (mint apron with big pockets), Ledger (friendly teal counting-bot on wheels, token counter on his chest), Mrs. Hen (a hen, obviously, with a garden hat), Grandpa (cardigan, slow smile).

## DNA map — what each source show contributed

For Will. Homage in structure and tone only — no copyrighted characters, names, or lines appear in the cartoon.

- **ReBoot** → *the setting's premise.* The heroes don't visit the system; they **live inside it** and are its small guardians. Scrolltown is a town inside the Great Scroll, and its citizens' everyday work — finding, writing down, counting, mapping, delivering — *is* the system's work, seen from inside.
- **Transformers** → *Ledger.* A friendly robot whose transformations (cart → bridge → stairs) exist for one reason: a neighbor needs help. The spectacle is never combat; the most heroic thing a big machine can do here is become a bridge and hold still while small friends cross.
- **The Land Before Time** → *the journey and the heart.* A gentle crossing of the Scroll Valley toward something that matters, at walking pace, with wonder instead of peril. The lost paper is Grandpa's story: the stakes are love and memory — stories keep people close — not danger.
- **Captain Planet** → *the gift structure and the closing hand-off.* Four small gifts (Finder, Sorter, Carrier, Keeper) that are each insufficient alone and exactly enough combined. The fix only happens when they combine — and the closing line hands the power to the viewer in our own words: **"The scroll is yours."**
- **Chip 'n Dale Rescue Rangers** → *the crew and the case.* A tiny specialist crew, a neighbor with a problem, a case-of-the-day shape ("Crew! We have a case!"), and solutions built from ingenuity and teamwork. Nobody is too small to help; the permit matters because Mrs. Hen matters.
- **Mister Rogers' Neighborhood** → *the frame and the tempo.* A kid narrator who talks straight to the viewer at the open and the close, names feelings plainly ("It's okay to feel mixed-up. Grown-ups feel mixed-up too."), moves slowly and kindly, and rides a little vehicle between neighborhoods — here a **number-cart**, because in Scrolltown the neighborhoods are coordinates: every stop is an address, and home is 1.1.1.
