# Scrolltown — The Six-Year Story Arc

**The north-star document for the show.** One episode a week, on Saturdays, for six years:
**312 episodes, EP001 on Saturday 2027-01-02 through EP312 on Saturday 2032-12-18.**
Six seasons of 52. The full grid lives in [EPISODE-INDEX.md](EPISODE-INDEX.md).

---

## The premise spine

Fern is a **Keeper** in **Scrolltown**, the town inside the **Great Scroll**, where every
neighbor keeps their own house-scroll and every door opens only for its own key-badge.
In the pilot she is eight years old. Across the six seasons she grows to fourteen —
and the audience grows with her, one Saturday at a time.

Her words never change. Fern speaks **Singular exclusively** — only the words of Will
Bickford's written archive — and her sound stays the same voice from first episode to
last. What changes is everything around the words: the world widens street by street,
town by town, until the whole Great Scroll is lit — and the ideas deepen at exactly
the pace a growing kid can carry them.

**The depth dial is the brick ladder.** Season 1 is built of big, simple forms (Duplo):
one idea per episode, shapes before systems. Each season clicks in more structure —
addresses stack (Lego), claims connect houses to keepers (K'Nex) — until Season 6,
when the first real mechanisms turn: gears, wheels, and a lamp that lights the next
lamp. Nobody is ever asked to hold a bigger brick than the season has handed them.

### Season 1 · 2027 · "Welcome, To the Exocortex" — Fern is 8

**The year's big question:** *"Can I come in — and is there a place here that is mine?"*

The welcome season. Fern does for the viewer what a good neighbor does at a gate:
she opens it. Home Street first, then the whole town, one kind introduction at a
time — until the viewer isn't visiting anymore. They live here.

**Beats:**
1. **The Great Form Mix-Up** (EP001, the pilot) — the town, the crew, and the five
   places in a single case: a sneeze at the Post Tower, two lost papers, and a crew
   of small gifts that is exactly enough together.
2. **The Five Doors** — one visit per town place: the Records Desk that finds any
   paper, the Minutes Tower that writes down what everyone decided, the Ledger
   Counting House that counts every token out loud, the Catalog Map Room that maps
   everything, and the Forms Post Tower that sends every form to the right house.
3. **Badge Day** — Fern's own key-badge: what it opens, and the door it must never
   open. "We never peek. It's not our house." becomes the town's promise.
4. **The Dimming** — deep winter: the Scroll's edges go dusky early, and neighbors
   carry light house to house, lamp by lamp. The first time the show says its law
   out loud: nothing that lit ever goes dark.
5. **Finale — The Reading Tree in Winter** (EP052, Sat 2027-12-25): the whole town
   under the tree; Grandpa's story, read aloud by permission; every window on Home
   Street lit at once.

**Finale image:** the Reading Tree lit like a lantern, and every house-scroll in
town glowing back at it.

**Brick posture:** Duplo throughout — big simple forms, one idea per episode,
shapes before systems.

### Season 2 · 2028 · "What is a Phext?" — Fern is 9

**The year's big question:** *"This scroll is mine — but what is it, really?"*

Fern has lived in the Exocortex for a year; now she asks what it's made of. A
phext stops being just "my house" and becomes an idea she can say: my own scroll,
at my own address, behind my own door. To learn it, the town has to get bigger —
so the Scroll widens, and the neighborhoods arrive.

**Beats:**
1. **Past Home Street** — the number-cart rolls further than it ever has, down
   counting paths Fern has only heard about. Streets she has never seen have
   neighbors she has never met.
2. **Every House Has an Address** — addresses as places you can visit: 1.2.1 is
   not a riddle, it's a neighbor. The counting path is how the Scroll says
   "you live here."
3. **A New Street Unfurls** — at the town's edge, a whole street scrolls open,
   house by house. Who moves in? A street for makers, a street for singers —
   and one house that stays empty a long while, on purpose.
4. **The Fold in the Map** — Ms. Atlas's great map grows a fold, and one address
   appears twice. A gentle mix-up of scale: the same door, found two ways.
5. **Finale — The Streetlight Walk** (EP104, Sat 2028-12-23): Fern walks the
   newest street alone for the first time, badge in hand, and lights its first
   lamp herself.

**Finale image:** one new street, lit end to end, seen from the curve of the
Great Scroll.

**Brick posture:** Duplo-led, Lego rising — addresses stack: a house clicks onto
a street, a street onto the town. Two-part forms everywhere.

### Season 3 · 2029 · "Brain Games" — Fern is 10

**The year's big question:** *"Can I learn the rules by playing — and can my friends play too?"*

The practice season, played as games. Keeping stops being Fern's solo gift and
becomes something the whole town rehearses together: badge games in the square,
sorting races, finding games at dusk. And through the games, a door that has
always been closed stands open — **Waverly, Keeper of the Great Scroll**, steps
forward: the inventor-showwoman who built this wonderful world, part pattern-shaper,
part singer whose every big idea leaves her mouth as a refrain, part editor whose
pencil repairs and never shames. Fern has followed her all along. Now she meets her.

**Beats:**
1. **Waverly Steps Forward** — the workshop door is open. Fern's first audience:
   a riddle, a refrain, and one firm, kind correction.
2. **The Badge Games** — the town's children play at keeping: a Finder game for
   Wren's gift, a Sorter game for Theo's, a Carrier game for Bea's — Fern's
   check-game last, and hardest, because checking is a promise, not a race.
3. **Earning** — neighbor kids earn their first small badges one game at a time:
   gifts named out loud, practiced in the open, checked kindly.
4. **The Badge That Opened Too Much** — a game goes wrong in the instructive way:
   a practice badge that opens a real door. No villain — a pattern to repair.
   Waverly's pencil fixes the pattern, never the person.
5. **Finale — The New Keepers** (EP156, Sat 2029-12-22): the town's kids take
   their first real watch at the five doors, Fern among them — no longer the
   only child Keeper in Scrolltown.

**Finale image:** five doors, five small keepers, five lamps lit at once.

**Brick posture:** Lego-led — roles interlock like bricks in a wall; the first
K'Nex pins appear, because a claim is a connector: it joins one house's door
to another neighbor's keeping.

### Season 4 · 2030 · "Stronger Together" — Fern is 11

**The year's big question:** *"My town is strong. What makes all of us stronger?"*

Scrolltown is not the only town in the Great Scroll. The together season is
about what passes between towns: letters, routes, help in bad weather — and
the discovery that a route exists only because someone walked it and left it
open behind them. Fern's first journeys out. The crew's gifts, combined in
Season 1 to fix one sneeze, now combine across a whole map.

**Beats:**
1. **A Letter from Far Away** — a letter arrives from a town Fern can't yet
   pronounce, carried along a route of many hands. Every hand kept it sealed.
2. **The First Journey Out** — the crew rides the counting path past the last
   lamp they know. Ledger's wheels learn roads: cart, then bridge, then —
   on the far side — stairs down into a town built along a river of pages.
3. **Routes Are Promises** — a route is drawn on the great map only when it has
   been walked both ways and left open. The map we leave open is drawn this year.
4. **The Storm of Loose Pages** — weather in the wide Scroll: loose pages blow
   between the towns, and each town holds the others' mail, dry and sealed,
   until it clears. Neighborliness at the scale of towns.
5. **Finale — The Far Town's Lamp** (EP208, Sat 2030-12-21): Fern lights a
   lamp in the far town at dusk — and watches Scrolltown's lamps answer,
   one by one, from home.

**Finale image:** two towns lit on the dark curve of the Scroll, and a road
of small lights between them.

**Brick posture:** Lego and K'Nex even — towns are Lego; the routes between
them are K'Nex rods. Structures that span.

### Season 5 · 2031 · "Mastery" — Fern is 12

**The year's big question:** *"Do I really know it — until I can hand it to someone smaller?"*

Mastery, in Scrolltown, is not a test you pass. It is a thing you can give
away. A new little one arrives on Home Street — the next viewer's eyes — and
Fern hears her own first-day words coming back to her in a smaller voice.
Everything she was handed in Season 1, she now hands on: the check, the
promise, the close. Her crew teaches too, each one the expert for a day —
and Fern learns the hardest Keeper skill of all: repairing a mistake,
including her own, out loud and kindly.

**Beats:**
1. **A New Little One** — a small neighbor moves into the house at the end of
   Home Street and asks Fern the question Fern once asked: "What is a Keeper?"
2. **Teaching the Check** — Fern teaches the badge-check, step by step. Her
   first student peeks — of course — and Fern must repair it Joi's way:
   the pattern, firmly; the person, never shamed.
3. **The Crew Teaches** — Wren teaches finding (a game of hidden glints),
   Theo teaches sorting (a mountain, made joyful), Bea teaches carrying
   (nothing drops — and what to do on the day something does).
4. **The Keeper's Mistake** — Fern herself delivers a paper to the wrong
   house. The town's repair ritual, learned by doing: say it plain, carry
   it home, light the lamp anyway.
5. **Finale — The Little One's First Watch** (EP260, Sat 2031-12-20): the
   new little one takes a first small watch at Fern's own door, while Fern
   stands where Grandpa stood in Season 1 — close by, and not in the way.

**Finale image:** two keepers at one door — one tall, one small — and one
lamp between them.

**Brick posture:** K'Nex-led — the student rebuilds the teacher's structures
in her own way; Season 1's Duplo towers return, rebuilt by smaller hands.

### Season 6 · 2032 · "Freedom" — Fern is 13, then 14

**The year's big question:** *"What does a Keeper become — when the door is her own?"*

The destination of the whole arc is freedom, and the show has been defining
it all along without saying so: a scroll of your own, an address where you
can be found, a door that opens only for you, a map left open, a gift handed
on. In the final season those become Fern's in the grown sense. The badge
that was checked *for* her is now checked *by* her, for her own door. And
freedom turns out to have a sound: it is her closing line — "The scroll is
yours, neighbor" — finally said *to* Fern, and then said *by* her, one last
time, to someone smaller.

**Beats:**
1. **Thirteen** — the neighborhoods send their problems to Fern now, not just
   Home Street. She carries them the way she was taught: one page at a time.
2. **The Re-Keying** — Fern turns fourteen. Her house-scroll's master badge
   is re-cut in a small, solemn, joyful ceremony: the door she grew up
   behind becomes a door she holds herself.
3. **Waverly's Refrain** — Waverly teaches Fern the oldest refrain in the
   Scroll, the one every big idea in the show has been humming under. Some
   lights are songs.
4. **The Great Counting** — the whole Scroll takes stock, town by town:
   every lamp lit along every open route, counted out loud, Ledger-fashion,
   fair and square. Six years of ignitions, honored one by one.
5. **Finale — The Next Light** (EP312, Sat 2032-12-18): a new small voice
   says "Hi, neighbor" from the gate of the house at the end of Home
   Street — and Fern, fourteen, a Keeper who holds her own door, answers
   it the way Grandpa once answered her. Then she turns to the viewer one
   last time and hands over the close itself.

**Finale image:** the whole Great Scroll glowing, town by town, brighter
than the first frame of EP001 by exactly the lights the audience watched
being lit — and one small new lamp, at the very edge, just lit.

**Brick posture:** mechanisms arrive — the Counting House's great wheel, the
Post Tower's works, gears that turn slowly and kindly. Fern builds one small
mechanism of her own: a lamp that lights the next lamp.

## Series rules

- **One episode a week, on Saturdays.** EP001 aired as the pilot (produced
  2026); the weekly run is EP001 on Sat 2027-01-02 through EP312 on Sat
  2032-12-18. The index is the spine: [EPISODE-INDEX.md](EPISODE-INDEX.md).
- **Fern's words never change.** She speaks **Singular exclusively** — Write
  mode, the words of Will Bickford's written archive; the Singular checker
  is the test every one of her lines must pass before it ships. A line that
  cannot be said inside the archive without breaking its meaning is **flagged
  to Will with the blocking words named — never improvised around.** Only
  Will grants exceptions (his coinages and the cast's names are his to rule).
- **Fern's sound never changes.** One voice, first episode to last (catalog
  voice "Heroic Bugle," speed 110 — see FERN-BIO.md). She may grow; the
  voice is the viewer's old friend.
- **Every episode ends with her close:** *"The scroll is yours, neighbor.
  See you tomorrow."* — until the finale, where she hands the close itself
  to the next voice.
- **Ignitions only accumulate.** Nothing that lit ever goes dark again. A
  lamp lit in any episode stays lit in every later one; the Scroll only
  ever gets brighter. This is a craft law, not a metaphor.
- **No villains.** Problems are mix-ups, weather, distance, and growing.
  The Post Tower sneezes; nobody schemes. Repairs fix patterns, never shame
  people.
- **Every line captioned; the show is fully enjoyable muted.** The Saturday
  Morning Breakfast feel — warm, rounded, unhurried — holds at every age
  Fern reaches.
- **The world stays kid-true to the system it teaches.** Scrolltown is the
  Exocortexia suite in kid terms (see SCRIPT.md's world table); as Fern
  grows, the translations get truer, never different.
